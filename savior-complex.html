The One Who Needed Saving Was Me

By Nefe Clarke

At seven, I learned to grow up too soon.

I was raised in a home where performance was almost the only way to be seen. I became self-reliant early, but I was never responsible only for myself. I carried the feelings of everyone around me. I answered for things I had not done. I read the weather of the adults in my life, and I measured whether I was welcome by their mood. Peace was a stranger in that house. Chaos was the familiar face, and in time it began to feel like home.

That followed me into adulthood, and I never knew it was there.

I held myself responsible for other people’s moods, for their sorrow and their joy. I could not rest until I had made someone smile, even for a single minute. To me, that felt like victory. It was never about glory. Some part of me believed that if I could make them happy, if I could only do something, they would see my worth and never cast me aside. I wanted to be indispensable. I never stopped to ask at whose expense. It was a very costly bargain.

All the while I was showing up for others and rescuing others, and the one who needed rescuing was me.

We do not speak enough about losing ourselves. It does not always show. You can look radiant, you can smile, you can be perfectly composed, while within there is a tempest, a bottomless dark. No one sees the tears, the struggle, the anxiety, the fear of being abandoned. You doubt everything. You are never granted a single minute of peace. A war rages in your head, in your stomach and in your heart, and all you ask is to live through the next day.

This is part of why Dawn at Dusk was born. If someone had taken my hand and told me to pause, I might have known sooner that I never had to rescue anyone to be loved. I never had to perform to be worthy of love.

I am still finding my way home. And for now, the one who matters most is me.
